⚠️ High-Risk Dishes & Hidden Sources

Tree nuts are frequently concealed in sauces, garnishes, thickeners, and desserts:

🍝 Sauces, Pestos & Curries
  • Pesto: Traditionally made with pine nuts or walnuts.
  • Indian Curries & Korma: Often thickened with cashew or almond paste.
  • Mole & Romesco: Frequently contain almonds, walnuts, or hazelnuts.
  • Thai / Asian Sauces: Crushed nut garnishes & shared woks.
🥐 Bakery, Pastries & Desserts
  • Marzipan & Frangipane: Pure almond paste / almond meal.
  • Baklava, Nougat & Pralines: Packed with pistachios, walnuts, hazelnuts.
  • Macarons: Traditionally made with almond flour.
  • Artisan Breads: May contain walnut pieces or seed/nut blends.
🍸 Specialty Beverages & Liqueurs
  • Amaretto: Almond or apricot kernel base.
  • Frangelico: Hazelnut-infused liqueur.
  • Nocino: Unripe green walnut liqueur.
  • Cocktail Orgeat Syrup: Almond-based cocktail sweetener.
🍳 Kitchen Cross-Contact Hazards
  • Shared Fryers: Frying nut-crusted items in common oil.
  • Ice Cream & Gelato Scoops: Shared rinse water bins.
  • Cutting Boards & Knives: Chopping garnishes without sanitizing.
  • Buffets & Salad Bars: Tongs transferred between bowls.

🔍 Common Distinctions & Clarifications

Botanical distinctions for ingredients that have "nut" in the name or are commonly confused:

🥜 Peanuts
Peanuts are legumes (grow underground), not botanical tree nuts. However, severe cross-contact risk often occurs in processing facilities.
Verify Cross-Contact
🥥 Coconut
Botanically a drupe / fibrous one-seeded fruit. Classified as a tree nut by US FDA for labeling, but biologically distinct.
Botanical Fruit
🌱 Seeds (Sesame, Sunflower, Pumpkin, Chia)
Seeds are botanically distinct from tree nuts and are generally separate unless processed on shared machinery.
Generally Separate
🧂 Nutmeg, Butternut Squash & Water Chestnuts
Nutmeg is a spice seed (Myristica fragrans); Water chestnuts are aquatic tubers; Butternut is a squash vegetable. None are tree nuts.
Not a Tree Nut
Emergency Action Protocol (Anaphylaxis)

If accidental ingestion occurs and symptoms appear (difficulty breathing, swelling of throat/lips, severe hives, vomiting, or dizziness):

1
Inject Epinephrine (EpiPen) into the outer mid-thigh immediately. Hold for 3 seconds.
2
Call 911 immediately and state: "Anaphylactic allergic reaction to tree nuts."
3
Stay with the person and keep them lying flat (or seated if breathing is hard). Administer secondary dose in 5-15 min if needed.
