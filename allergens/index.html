Gideon Tong's Allergies

A quick reference for dining, catering, and event hosts.

Allergen Summary
I have an allergy to raw tree nuts (including almonds, pistachios, hazelnuts, pecans, and pine nuts) as well as powdered spices (such as turmeric, paprika, and dry seasoning blends) and capsaicin.

Common Dishes

Common culinary preparations where raw tree nuts, powdered spices, and capsaicin are frequently present:

Raw Nut Dishes & Garnishes
  • Salad & Grain Bowls: Raw walnuts, pecans, or slivered almonds tossed in salads and smoothie bowls.
  • Fresh Pestos & Cold Dips: Uncooked pine nut or walnut pestos, cold romesco, raw nut garnishes.
  • Raw Vegan Cheeses & Creams: Unheated cashew cheese, raw almond ricotta dips, cold-pressed oils.
  • Unbaked Desserts & Confections: Energy date/nut bars, unbaked raw cashew cheesecake crusts, unbaked marzipan.
Powdered Spices & Seasonings
  • Curries, Soups & Stews: Golden milk, yellow curries, and broths flavored with turmeric, cumin, or coriander.
  • Dry Rubs & Seasoning Blends: BBQ rubs, taco seasonings, fajita mixes, and dry spice blends.
  • Seasoned Snacks & Sides: Paprika-dusted fries, seasoned potato chips, spiced popcorn.
  • Dressings & Marinades: Ranch dressing mixes, spice-infused vinaigrettes, and dry powder marinades.
Capsaicin & Spicy Foods
  • Hot Sauces & Condiments: Sriracha, Tabasco, sambal oelek, harissa, chili crisp, buffalo sauce.
  • Spicy Regional Dishes: Thai spicy curries, Sichuan chili oil dishes, spicy salsas, enchiladas.
  • Spicy Broths & Noodles: Spicy ramen with rayu, kimchi jjigae, spicy hot pot broths.
  • Spicy Glazes & Drizzles: Hot honey drizzle, sweet chili glazes, spicy BBQ wings.
Sauces, Salsas & Mixed Blends
  • Fresh & Cooked Salsas: Salsas containing jalapeños, chili powders, and ground cumin.
  • Mole & Multi-Spice Sauces: Rich specialty sauces combining chili peppers, spices, and nuts.
  • Asian Stir-Fry Sauces: Pad Thai sauces, chili garlic pastes, and prepared curry pastes.
  • Charcuterie & Snack Trays: Spiced cured meats, chili-infused cheeses, seasoned crackers.

Common Distinctions & Clarifications

Key distinctions regarding raw nuts, spice forms, peppers, seeds, and commonly confused ingredients:

Raw vs. Baked / Cooked Nuts
The tree nut allergy specifically applies to raw and uncooked tree nuts (such as fresh raw nut garnishes, uncooked pestos, and unbaked vegan cashew creams).
Fresh Aromatics vs. Powdered Spices
The spice restriction focuses on dry ground/powdered spices and seasoning blends. Fresh whole garlic cloves and fresh onions are distinct from dehydrated powders.
Black & White Peppercorns vs. Capsaicin
Table black, white, and green peppercorns (Piper nigrum) contain piperine, not capsaicin, and are biologically unrelated to Capsicum chili peppers (jalapeños, cayenne, paprika).
Pink Peppercorns (Cashew Family)
Pink peppercorns are dried berries from the Brazilian pepper tree (Schinus terebinthifolia), which belongs to the cashew family (Anacardiaceae) and should be avoided.
Sichuan Peppercorns
Sichuan peppercorns are the husks of prickly ash shrubs (citrus family) and produce a numbing sensation from hydroxy-alpha-sanshool, containing no capsaicin.
Fresh Herbs & Leafy Greens
Fresh culinary herbs (such as fresh basil, cilantro, parsley, rosemary, thyme, and mint) are fresh leafy produce and are distinct from dry powdered seasoning blends.
Peanuts
Peanuts are legumes (grow underground), not botanical tree nuts. However, cross-contact risk can occur in shared processing facilities.
Coconut
Botanically a drupe / fibrous one-seeded fruit. Classified as a tree nut by US FDA for labeling, but biologically distinct from true tree nuts.
Seeds (Sesame, Sunflower, Pumpkin, Chia)
Culinary seeds (sesame, sunflower, pumpkin, chia, flax, poppy) are botanically distinct from tree nuts and are generally separate unless processed on shared machinery.
Nutmeg, Butternut Squash & Water Chestnuts
Nutmeg is a spice seed (Myristica fragrans); Water chestnuts are aquatic tubers; Butternut is a squash vegetable. None are botanical tree nuts.
